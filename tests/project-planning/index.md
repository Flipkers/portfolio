## **1. Project plan**

**Week 1 — Immersion and brief**

First I have a short call with the client. My goal is not just to fill in a brief, but to understand how the club positions itself: who it is for, how it differs from chain gyms, and what emotions it wants to create. That drives how the content and visuals are presented.

After that I lock the brief and the landing structure: blocks, goals, main CTAs. I usually sketch this in Miro so the client can see the overall logic of the page. Once it is agreed, I put together the project roadmap and put the tasks in Trello.

---

**Week 2 — Prototype and design**

I hand the designer a brief that includes the audience, the key messages, and examples of competitor sites. A couple of days later the designer shows a prototype, and I review it — checking that the blocks follow a logical order and that there is no information noise.

Then I send it to the client with an explanation of why the structure is the way it is (so it does not feel like “just a picture”). After the feedback I collect every change in one document and help prioritize them so we do not lose the schedule.

---

**Week 3 — Layout and setup**

Once the design is approved, the developer starts the layout. At this point I watch the pace and the communication, so nothing stalls while we wait on content or access.

In parallel we connect the lead forms and set up analytics (GA4, Meta Pixel, UTM), and check that everything is passed to the CRM correctly. I always ask the team and the client for test leads, to be sure the system works from start to finish.

---

**Week 4 — Content and launch**

We upload the content — texts, photos of the club, prices, offers. I check that the landing does not look “technically empty”: we add live photos, reviews, and a clear offer block.

Then we set up ads: first a small test budget, to check the “traffic → lead” link.

Finally we check load speed, responsiveness, and pixels. After the tests we launch the project to production and watch the first leads.

**Checkpoints:**

- End of week 1 — brief and structure approved.
- End of week 2 — design agreed.
- End of week 3 — landing built and tested.
- End of week 4 — site launched and the first leads in.

## **2. Control and communication**

**With the team:**

I record every task in Trello, and each card has a deadline and an owner. Every week there are two short syncs:

- Monday — the plan,
- Thursday — a status check.

I try to keep the focus off control for its own sake and on removing blockers: if the designer is waiting on content, I check with the copywriter; if the developer is waiting on access, I sort it out with the client.

**With the client:**

Once a week — a short 15–20 minute call that I initiate. I show the interim result: a link to the prototype, screenshots, a short video with an explanation. Between meetings — updates in Telegram: “design is ready”, “we launched an ad test”, without extra bureaucracy. The client always knows what stage we are at and what comes next.

## **3. Risk management**

If the developer delays the CRM integration by 2 days, I first find out what is blocking the task: a technical question or an external factor.

Inside the team I reshuffle priorities — for example we keep working on content and analytics so nobody sits idle.

I tell the client in advance, calmly and specifically:

“The integration will take two more days. We can launch the landing with a temporary lead form — all requests will go to email, and we will connect the CRM later. This will not affect how fast leads come in.”

That way the client sees that I have the situation under control, rather than making excuses.

## **4. Final result**

By the end of the month we should have:

- a fully finished, responsive landing with the CRM and analytics connected,
- ad campaigns launched (Yandex + VK),
- the first real leads, which can be measured and analyzed,
- a short report with the numbers and a proposal for scaling.

If the client is already getting inquiries and can see a clear plan for the next stage, the project went well.
