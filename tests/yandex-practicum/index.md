A PR person’s job is about communication, event announcements, press releases, and creating an information background for the brand. You have to talk a lot, speak well, and write well. A few thoughts here:

### Who is our audience?

- People who already work in, or have a degree in, marketing, psychology, or advertising.
- People who already know they want to try PR.
- People who have decided to change careers and are looking for new ways to grow
- Current university students who are not sure they want to work in the field of their diploma.

### Our value proposition and the goal of the course

1. Now we can shape our own value proposition. It will be roughly this:
- a short training period — 5 months
- partner internships already during the training (companies you can join)
- a price below the main competitors — 70 thousand instead of 100 thousand at Skillbox.
- real projects already during the training

The goal of our course is to bring beginners into the profession, give them the necessary base of knowledge, and sharpen their skills on real projects, so they can then start a career.

How to test the hypothesis

1. So we have 6 ways to test the hypothesis. How do we pick the effective ones from here?

**I think getting someone to pay is always the best option, because you cannot trust intentions. If a person has handed over money, that means they are at least interested. Even if the solution does not suit them, they can get the money back, and we get valuable feedback.**

Choosing the most effective hypothesis test

### Admitting the segment cannot pay

- We can set a benchmark for the webinar. Say conversion from webinar to purchase is 10% and we expect 200 people. Then if **50 people** sign up, we can already draw conclusions here, because not all 50 will make it to the webinar itself, and 10% of that is already fewer than 5 people.
- I would also start from our business model. Say producing the course costs 1 million rubles. At a course price of 70 thousand, we need at least 15 students who paid for the training to break even, and 15+ students to make a profit. But that is only the break-even point. We also need to define the point after which we admit the segment cannot pay. **Say up to 10 sales.** 10 sales is a loss, but it is far from zero and we can work with it to raise them to what we need. Anything less than that no longer interests us. Up to 10 sales — inability to pay.

### What to do if nothing worked?

First, be specific. What exactly went wrong? Our hypothesis can be split into several parts.

- funnel and marketing — we have few sales. We need to look at how many people were on the site. What was the conversion? How many reached the payment screen? **We look at the funnel and find the weak spots**
- the product and its value — we picked the wrong value proposition and it did not convince the audience. **We work on the product’s value**
- the user segment — we understood our audience poorly. **We work with the current segment or look for new user segments who might be interested**
- the course business model — the course does not pay for itself because it is long, the experts are expensive, it takes a long time, and so on. **We need to optimize, cut extra information, and work with the experts.**

💡 Overall conclusion — we need to invent other variations of this experiment, with a different funnel, different segments, and a different value, and repeat it several times. And if we still cannot make it work, then we should stop spending the shareholders’ money and focus on other products.
